# Project North Star

## One-sentence promise

The Agentic Engineering Primer is an accurate and useful technical guide for using AI coding tools: what to do, when to do it, what can go wrong, and how to verify the result.

## Users and jobs

The primer serves readers who need practical information about AI-assisted engineering:

- beginners who need safe first workflows
- practicing engineers who need reliable habits and verification loops
- team leads who need policy, permission, and rollout criteria

The job is to explain the workflow accurately enough that readers can apply it, test it, and decide when it does not fit.

## Success signals

The project is on track when:

- pages answer concrete reader questions without requiring vendor shopping first
- workflows include inputs, steps, verification, failure modes, and boundaries
- claims are qualified by evidence status or marked as perishable reference
- reference pages identify what must be checked against live sources
- readers can distinguish durable practice from fast-changing product detail

## Strategic bets

- Durable workflow guidance is more useful than live market coverage.
- Verification, context control, security boundaries, and learning discipline are the core skills.
- Plain technical writing is more trustworthy than persuasive positioning.
- Tool and model pages should support workflow decisions, not become the product.
- Examples should show procedure, judgment, and verification limits.

## Tone and editorial stance

The primer should convey accurate, useful information. It should not sell a transformation.

Prefer:

- direct statements over motivational framing
- procedures over slogans
- caveats over reassurance
- examples over claims of authority
- limits and failure modes over confidence language
- specific workflow facts over brand, hype, or market language

Avoid:

- sales copy
- hype words
- emotional goals
- broad promises of empowerment or productivity
- vendor-ranking drama
- copy that sounds like a product landing page
- simplifications that make the guidance less accurate

## Non-goals

The project is not:

- a comprehensive AI tools database
- a buyer's guide organized around vendor rankings
- a benchmark or pricing tracker
- a template catalog
- a product-marketing site
- a motivational guide to adopting AI

## Constraints

- Core pages should age slowly.
- Fast-changing material belongs in reference pages with freshness metadata.
- Public guidance must preserve security, privacy, and verification caveats.
- Tables should clarify decisions; they should not become dense market matrices.
- Claims that depend on current vendor behavior need live-source checks or removal.
- New content should reduce ambiguity for the reader, not broaden the scope.

## Off-track signals / kill criteria

Re-scope or rewrite when:

- a page reads like it is trying to persuade the reader to adopt AI
- tool/model selection becomes the center of the page without workflow context
- exact prices, scores, quotas, or rankings appear in core curriculum
- copy promises confidence without specifying verification
- examples omit failure modes or review steps
- new templates or reference pages add maintenance burden without a distinct instructional pattern

## Sprint decision filter

When choosing work, prefer the task that most improves one of these:

1. clarity of instruction
2. verification quality
3. context-control discipline
4. security or permission boundaries
5. evidence labeling and caveats
6. reduction of stale or promotional copy

If two options are similar, choose the smaller edit that makes the guidance more factual and easier to apply.
